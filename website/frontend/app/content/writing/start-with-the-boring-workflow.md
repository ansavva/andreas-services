When people ask me where to start with AI, they’re usually hoping I’ll name something exciting. I almost never do.

## Boring is where the hours hide

The flashy projects — a chatbot, a “copilot,” a slick demo — get the attention. But the hours your team actually loses are hiding somewhere dull: re-typing the same data between two systems, triaging a shared inbox, formatting the weekly report, tagging support tickets.

Nobody brags about automating those. That’s exactly why they’re still costing you money.

## A quick test

Pick the task that makes someone on your team sigh every time it lands. Ask three questions:

- Does it follow rules more than it needs judgment?
- Does it happen often enough that small savings compound?
- Would nothing catch fire if a draft needed a quick human check?

Three yeses and you’ve probably found your first automation.

## Why it works

Boring workflows are predictable, and predictable is exactly what these systems are good at. You get a fast win, your team starts trusting the tools, and you build the muscle for the bigger stuff later — on the back of something that already paid for itself.

Start boring. Boring compounds.
